S9 · One command for the whole chain, and the server runbook

One command runs select → train ×2 → eval ×2 → returns ×2 → verify on real LIBERO rollouts, and traces the verdict back through checkpoints, subsets and versions to the 20 raw HDF5 files, with no stand-in left on the path. A runbook turns the four server-dependent ACs into exact commands, with inputs by id and disk estimates.

ACs 3/3 met Depends on S0–S8 Scale smoke (not attribution-grade)

Why

"Evaluate both policies as in Step 2. Apply the macro-KS test: did the time-to-success distribution change at all?" — PoC §9.1 Step 4, p. 19
"Every eval rollout should be tied back to that checkpoint." — PoC §2.1 item 1, p. 3

The brief's constraint: with no server yet, "mark every server-dependent AC explicitly as 'pending server', with exactly what will be run there". This story replaces the data+lineage feature's eval stand-in with the real stage end to end, and writes that runbook.

Divergences and limitations

  • Smoke scale only. 2-step ACT policies and 2 rollouts per arm. The chain proves wiring and lineage, not an effect.
  • The returns runs sit beside the verdict, not on its upstream path. Both consume the same rollouts entities; the verdict needs the (T, E) pairs, which are in the rollouts. They appear in the chain graph, not in trace(verify).
  • The real store was written once in round 2 (this chain, 10 runs). Every other MLflow-writing test used runs.scratch_lineage_copy; the real mlflow.db sha256 stayed 7845d8d7… through the refactor and the summarizer's re-runs.
  • Disk and time estimates are inferred from measured per-frame and per-checkpoint costs on this Mac. Server GPU throughput, batch 50 in GPU memory (fallback: one smaller divisor of 50 for every arm and seed) and CUDA determinism are unmeasured.
