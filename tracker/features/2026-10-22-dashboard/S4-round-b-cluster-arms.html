S4 · Round B for discovered clusters

A discovered cluster becomes a retraining test: remove it, retrain, and a stated rule turns the results into confirmed, not confirmed or refuted.

ACs 5/5 Depends on S2 Data scores SYNTHETIC, no real results yet

In plain words

A cluster found from scores is only a guess about what hurts. To test it, we retrain the policy without those recordings and compare with the original policy, in three training seeds. The expectation (for example "removing these should improve speed") is written down before training, from the estimate. Afterwards a fixed rule says confirmed (the expectation held in at least 2 of 3 seeds and no seed contradicted it), not confirmed, or refuted (the opposite happened). The test can also remove an equal-sized random set instead, as a control.

Terms are defined in the glossary of the main report.

Why

PoC §2.1 item 5 describes leave-one-group-out ablations as the coarse, unambiguous reference; §4.4 gives the two-step verdict used to decide whether a change is real; §9.1 Step 4: "Influence estimates can be wrong. This step turns them from claims into verified results."

"Leave-one-group-out ablations. Drop one data source or task group, retrain, and measure the change." — §2.1 item 5, p. 3

Divergences and limitations

  • The matched-random option costs 45 jobs, not the 33 the plan first counted; a flag-sized change would give the cheaper shape if the owner wants it.
  • The expectation comes from the cluster's estimated reading, which is uncorrected for metrics and selection. A noise-driven reading can become an expectation that then fails to confirm. It can give "not confirmed" or "refuted", not a false "confirmed" (inferred).
  • The rule generalises "2 of 3" to ceil(2n/3) of n seeds.
  • Real verdicts need the server; the verdicts in the demo are SYNTHETIC and say so in the status text.
