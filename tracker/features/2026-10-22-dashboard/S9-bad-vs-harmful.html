S9 · Bad-looking vs harmful (estimated)

The spec's "most compelling output": which recordings look bad but do not hurt the policy, and which look clean but do, as a table, a chart and example videos.

ACs 7/7 Depends on S1, S2 Data scores SYNTHETIC, no real results yet

In plain words

Intuition says bad data hurts. The spec says the interesting finding is where intuition fails: damaged-looking recordings that do not hurt, and clean-looking ones that do. We know exactly which 91 recordings were damaged, so "looks bad" is a fact, not a guess. This story compares that with the influence scores: for each of the seven damage types and each metric, does the type's average harm differ from the clean data's, with an honest interval? It also lists the clean recordings with the highest harm, and shows a damaged episode beside its clean original. Everything here is an estimate from scores; S10 plans the retraining that would test it.

Terms are defined in the glossary of the main report.

Why

PoC §9.2 (p. 19) calls this the most compelling output; §9.1 Step 1 says screening verdicts are stored so they can be compared with what actually happened. The owner chose the planted labels over building a new screening method. CUPID's Fig. 4 caption (p. 8) is published precedent that quality and effect can come apart.

"The most compelling output is where screening and outcome attribution disagree: episodes that look bad but do not hurt the policy, and episodes that look clean but do." — §9.2, p. 19

Visual

Secondary layer (SYNTHETIC scores, real descriptors and labels, so these counts do not depend on the invented scores): the any-of-H1–H8 filter flags 438 of 960 episodes and 89 of the 91 planted (precision 0.20, recall 0.98). Per heuristic, flagged/planted: H5 26/26 and H6 13/13 are precise; H1 86/12, H2 81/15, H4 91/22.

Divergences and limitations

  • Never "harmless". A non-excluding interval is stated as "no detectable harm" with its bounds, and "not proof of no harm".
  • Uncorrected. 7 types × 5 metrics = 35 readings; about 1.75 false readings are expected by chance at 5%. Holm across the 5 metrics is shown as a tighter, partial correction. The first method (percentile bootstrap) called 9.3% of null cells, so it was replaced with Welch (5.9%).
  • 13 episodes per type is a small group (1.4%); an all-null result is inconclusive, not reassuring.
  • The demo scores are invented, so the quadrant counts show the view, not a fact about any policy.
  • The union "any of H1–H8" filter flags 46% of the data, because nine rankers each flag about 9.5%; the per-heuristic rows are the informative layer.
