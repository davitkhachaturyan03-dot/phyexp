S4 · Complete the verification step: helpful arms, controls, opposite curations, a gate-aware summary

The experiment design now has every arm the spec asks for (harmful removal, helpful removal, random controls, both curation modes), a head-to-head between two curation targets, and a summary that respects the two-step verdict.

ACs 6/6 Depends on S3 Glossary report.html#glossary

In plain words

Finding harmful recordings is only a claim until you test it. The spec's test: remove the recordings the method calls most harmful, retrain the robot model, and see whether it gets better than after removing the same number of random recordings. Then do the reverse with the most helpful ones, which should make things worse. The ported code had only the first half and compared each arm only with the original model, never with a random control.

This story adds the missing arms and comparisons to the job chain, adds a command that summarises a whole experiment across training seeds (a seed is one random starting point for training; repeating with several shows what is luck), and adds the spec's "opposite curations" test: curate the same dataset once for speed and once for reliability and check that each curated model wins on its own metric. It also builds both ways of choosing what to remove, per task and across all tasks, as the owner decided.

The analyzer found one real defect here: the summary counted a metric's direction even when the first step of the spec's two-step verdict (the macro-KS gate, a test of whether the whole distribution of finishing times changed at all) had not fired. That is fixed and tested. All the figures on this page come from planted synthetic verdicts; no model has been retrained, so there is no real Step 4 result yet.

Terms (macro-KS gate, Holm, seed, arm, per-task vs global curation, family W, bootstrap, n_boot) are in the report glossary.

Why

PoC §9.1 Step 4 and §2.6 define the counterfactual test, §4.4 defines the two-step verdict, and §4.5 defines "same dataset, opposite curations". The owner's Q1 ruling (recorded in the brief) was to build both curation modes with the per-task one as the headline.

"Remove the top-k most harmful episodes and retrain. As a control, remove k random episodes and retrain." — §9.1 Step 4, p. 19
"First the macro-KS gate ... Then each metric's bootstrap CI gives the direction." — §4.4, p. 10 (quoted from the plan's reading of the page)

Example

A planted case (synthetic): the "most harmful removed" arm beats its random control on F(30), the chance of finishing within 30 s, in seeds 0 and 1 but shows no clear difference in seed 2. The summary row reads: T,T,0 | treated better in 2/3 seeds, control better in 0/3, gate not fired in 0/3; expectation (treated_better) met in 2/3. Then the round-2 case: three seeds where the macro-KS gate never fired but every metric looked significant on its own. Before the fix this counted as "expectation met in 3/3" (analyzer measured it); now it is g,g,g, supports 0.

Cost. The default chain is large. The first-run decision for the owner, with the whole table, is in the report's open decisions.

Divergences and limitations

  • Per-task versus global curation (owner-approved, Q1: "Both, per-task headline"). The PoC's literal text is global top-k; the ported code was per-task (grounded in ATHENA §3.2, not in the PoC). Both are built; per-task is the reported headline, global is reported alongside. This roughly doubles the server's retraining arms.
  • Each treated arm is compared with every random draw (2 draws) of its family, each a separate row, so the control's own variability is visible. The new comparisons use family W only (the declared family); the comparisons against the base model keep families W and O.
  • The summary does not re-test: the two-step verdict is vlapoc.verdict's. Expected directions (harm removed: treated better; help removed: treated worse; HRT arm wins HRT, F(30) arm wins F(30)) are written in step4.py before any real verdict.
  • Global arms rank the all-rollout column and remove N = k × 20 = 100 episodes to match the per-task total; the claim that per-task columns cannot be ranked globally is a design reason (inferred), not measured.
  • Not available here: any real retrained-arm verdict (server).
