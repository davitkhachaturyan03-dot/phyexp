S6 · Make the chain testable and portable

The job chain can be submitted from any cluster account by copying one settings file, cannot pick up another run's results, and creates its own log folder.

ACs 7/7 Depends on — Glossary report.html#glossary

In plain words

The whole experiment is submitted to the cluster's scheduler as a chain of jobs. SLURM is the scheduler most shared GPU clusters use: you submit jobs, they wait in a queue and run on a free machine. sbatch is the command that submits one job, and --dependency=afterok means "start this job only after the named jobs finished successfully". The ported scripts had the owner's cluster folders written into them, always picked up "the latest" finished run by name (which could be an older experiment's), and could not be pointed at a throwaway store for testing.

This story moves every machine-specific setting into one example file, makes run names resolve only inside their own experiment, lets every command use a scratch store, and keeps temporary files inside the project.

In round 1 the analyzer also found that the chain never created its log folder: SLURM opens a job's output file before the job starts and does not create missing folders, so on a fresh cluster account every job would have failed silently. That is fixed and tested with a stubbed sbatch (nothing was submitted anywhere).

Terms (SLURM, sbatch, afterok, MLflow, lineage) are in the report glossary.

Why

The brief's lead 10 (hard-coded environment) and lead 9 (lineage and records) asked whether the cluster paths were acceptable and whether every stage could be re-verified without touching the real store. PoC §2.1 item 1 requires each run to be tied to exact inputs, and a name that can silently resolve to another chain's run breaks that.

"Every training run should be tied to exact dataset versions (content hashes), episode IDs, ... the config, the git SHA, and the resulting checkpoints." — §2.1 item 1, p. 3

Divergences and limitations

  • A dry run without slurm/site.env falls back to the example file; a real submission without one exits with an instruction.
  • That #SBATCH directive lines do not expand variables is from the sbatch manual (inferred here, not re-read), which is why the chain passes the flags on the command line.
  • The chain refuses to submit from a dirty tree and each job refuses a different commit (slurm/guard.sh); that was already true.
  • Not available here: running the chain for real on the server, and SQLite behaviour under concurrent jobs (out of scope, needs the server).
