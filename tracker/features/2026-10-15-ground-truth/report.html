Ground truth (injection experiment)

There is now a LIBERO dataset (v3) in which 91 of 960 candidate episodes carry a known corruption, seven types of 13 each, with the answer key kept apart, a single loader that keeps methods blind to it, and a scorer for "how many did the method recover". The measured catch: cheap label-free statistics already find every type (the hardest, time_shift, at AUROC 0.848), so a method has to beat those per type, not chance.

Date 2026-10-15 → 2026-10-17 Milestone Ground truth (§10, p. 20) Stories 6 · ACs 44/44 Analyzer rounds 3 Status verified policy harm pending server

Headline (AC5.9). No corruption type is in detectability band (c), "not found by any heuristic". Six of the seven types are found at AUROC 0.95 or higher by a label-free one-line statistic. time_shift is the hardest, at 0.848. Any method's recovery must therefore be compared per type, against the best heuristic for that type (the table in the Demo section), not against chance. This is an owner-approved limitation under ruling A, not a defect left behind.

Not shown here. Whether a corruption actually hurts a trained policy, and whether removing the corrupted episodes helps, needs SmolVLA training and rollouts on a server (runbook items GT-R1 to GT-R6). Nothing in this report claims it.

The table to beat

For each corruption type, the best any of eight label-free heuristics achieves, measured on v3 (13 injected against 869 clean per type). Both numbers matter: AUROC says the injected episodes are generally ranked above clean ones; recall@13 says how many land in the very top 13, which is the PoC's "recovered 90%" currency.

TypeBest heuristicAUROCRecall@13Best recall@13 over H1–H8Inherent or an artifact of construction
action_noiseH4 smoothness1.0001.0001.000Largely inherent: noise raises step-to-step change. Independent per-step noise is RoboDrop's own setup
dropped_framesH5 duplicate_frame_pairs1.0001.0001.000Held frames are exact duplicates; clean data has none (0 of 199,340 pairs). Real cameras behave this way too
idle_paddingH2 idle_steps (H5 ties)1.0001.0001.000Idle count: inherent. Duplicate frames: a construction artifact (limitation C)
instruction_swapH6 instruction_mismatch1.0001.0001.000Construction (limitation B): provenance stays true, so the instruction contradicts task_name
gripper_releaseH8 post_release_share_vs_task0.9640.0000.017 (H3, AUROC 0.505)Inherent: an early release lengthens the open tail
detourH7 length_vs_task0.9600.1540.154Inherent: +40 steps against the task's norm
time_shiftH7 shortest first0.8480.4620.462Residual of the trim: −40 steps; found only when the parent was not an unusually long demo

Why gripper_release has AUROC 0.964 and recall@13 of 0.000

The two numbers answer different questions. AUROC is the chance that a random injected episode outranks a random clean one, so it rewards being "ranked high". Recall@13 asks whether the 13 injected episodes are inside the top 13 of 882. Under H8 the 13 injected episodes rank 16, 17, 20, 23, 26, 27, 28, 29, 39, 48, 50, 78 and 97 (measured). All are in the top 100, but 15 clean episodes, whose post-release tails are naturally long (task-relative shares of 0.24–0.57, analyzer-measured), sit above the best injected one. So the heuristic finds the type in the AUROC sense and misses it in the recall sense. A future method should be judged on both, and the PoC's headline, "recovered 90% of the injected bad episodes", is a recall statement.

Disputes and owner rulings

Round 1, the detectability dispute (dispute-r1.md). The question put to the owner: "Ground truth dispute: 5 of 7 corruption types are found by one-line statistics. Which detectability leaks should be removed in the single re-derivation?" The analyzer recommended Option A. Ruling A, verbatim (2026-10-17): "A. Fix time_shift only (rec.)". That means:

  • The time_shift idle tail became a trim: observations 0 … T−41 paired with actions 40 … T−1, the length change (−40) recorded in the label. Measured effect: idle_steps AUROC on time_shift went from 1.000 to 0.445.
  • Everything else was kept, and recovery is reported per type against the heuristic baselines, saying plainly which types test anything beyond one-line statistics.
  • B (hiding provenance, correlated noise) and C (re-rendered idle) were not built; D arose later (below).

The ruling was final and the analyzer did not re-open it. Cost: one more full-scale derivation (v3, 562 s, free disk 86 to 78 GiB).

Pending server

Whether the injected data hurts a trained policy needs training and rollouts that this Mac cannot do at scale. artifacts/server-runbook.md lists them in order, none run: GT-R1 SmolVLA on v3/candidate with the evaluation feature's AC8.5 configuration; GT-R2 evaluate each checkpoint with the AC5.8 protocol and a batch plan equal to the clean arm's; GT-R3 returns; GT-R4 the verdict v3/candidate against v0/candidate (macro-KS gate, then per-metric intervals; a finding, not an acceptance condition); GT-R5 (optional) the oracle arm, 869 episodes with the 91 removed; GT-R6 the hand-off, scoring a later method per type against the table to beat. LIBERO has no ghost-event rule, so the gripper-release "drops" hypothesis can only show as lower success, F(τ) or timeouts. Estimated total disk 50–95 GB plus headroom (inferred).

Known issue for the attribution feature. vlapoc.runs.train_run and eval_run bypass open_for_method and assert_blind. That is right for this feature's own training arms, but a later method that trains policies (datamodels, CUPID) could pass a ground-truth-derived subset to train_run and not be refused, and train_run("v1/candidate", …) would run on the fenced v1. The attribution feature must route its training inputs through the loader.

How to read the code

The pipeline: choose episodes, corrupt them while writing a new dataset version, let a method screen it without seeing the answer, then score its ranking against the answer key.

  1. tracker/features/2026-10-15-ground-truth/brief.md, "Owner decisions" and "Owner rulings": clipping, scope, blindness now, and ruling A.
  2. vlapoc/corruptions.py: the seven types as pure functions. Read make_edit, then action_noise (simplest), time_shift (changes length, ruling A) and clip_actions (decision 1). Each type's params says whether each parameter is quoted, measured or inferred.
  3. vlapoc/derive.py, EpisodeEdit and derive_version: applies edits while writing the new dataset in the parent's order, writes labels.parquet and parents.parquet, and refuses to register a version that is not blind.
  4. vlapoc/lineage.py: compare_blind, check_blind, assert_blind (what counts as leaking an edit: the instruction is content, provenance is bookkeeping) and write_fence (v1 fenced for a positional leak, v2 as superseded).
  5. vlapoc/groundtruth.py: select_injection, build_edits and derive_injection (the v3 command), then recovery_metrics, score_recovery, leak_check, and last retire_version.
  6. vlapoc/method_data.py: open_for_method, the one door a method uses.
  7. vlapoc/methods/heuristics.py: H1–H8 through that door; scan_episodes and compute are the core. Their per-type scores are the table to beat.
  8. The verifiers in story order, each docstring holding its run command: scripts/verify_corruptions_s1.py, verify_derive_gt_s2.py, verify_method_boundary_s3.py, verify_injection_s4.py, verify_recovery_s5.py with s5_recovery_table.py, and verify_consumers_s6.py.
  9. tracker/features/2026-10-15-ground-truth/artifacts/server-runbook.md: what still needs the server.
