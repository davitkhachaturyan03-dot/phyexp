S5 · Recovery scoring and the table to beat

A scorer that turns any method's ranking into "how many of the injected episodes did it find", and eight label-free heuristics run through the blind loader to set the bar. No corruption type is out of reach of a cheap statistic, so a method must beat that bar per type, not chance.

ACs 9/9 Depends on S3, S4 Code vlapoc/groundtruth.py, vlapoc/methods/heuristics.py

In plain words

This story built the scorer and the baseline. The scorer takes any method's ranking of episodes from most to least suspicious and reports how many damaged episodes it found. The baseline is eight cheap one-line statistics (for example, "how long is the episode?") that need no learning and no answer key. The key result: they already find all seven kinds of damage, with AUROC (a score from 0.5, no better than guessing, to 1.0, perfect, for how well a ranking puts damaged episodes above clean ones) from 0.848 for time_shift, the hardest, up to 1.000. So a real method must beat them kind by kind, not just beat chance. One caution: gripper_release has an AUROC of 0.964 but a recall@13 (the share of the 13 damaged episodes found inside the top 13 ranked) of 0, because its damaged episodes rank high but not at the very top.

Unfamiliar term? See the glossary in the overall report. Technical detail follows below.

Headline (AC5.9). With eight label-free statistics (H1–H8), no corruption type is in band (c) "not found by any heuristic". Six of seven types are found at AUROC 0.95 or higher. time_shift is the hardest, at 0.848 (the child is shorter than its task's norm). So a method's recovery number means something only per type, against that type's best heuristic, not against chance (AUROC 0.5). This is an owner-approved limitation under ruling A (with B and C), not a defect fixed here. The round-2 table said gripper_release and time_shift were "not found"; that was an artifact of the first six heuristics lacking task normalisation (analyzer round 2).

Why

The point of planting ground truth is a sentence like "the method recovered 90% of the injected bad episodes". That needs a scorer, and it needs to know what a trivial method already achieves, otherwise a high recovery number can be a cheap statistic in disguise. RoboDrop scores with AUROC and best balanced accuracy, and PoC §5 lists intrinsic-quality heuristics (smoothness, redundancy) as the baseline family.

This turns "the method says X" into "the method recovered 90% of the injected bad episodes." — §2.1 item 4, p. 3
Inject 5–10% corrupted episodes and measure how many the method ranks near the top. — §2.5, p. 5
We then assess how well these scores separate reliable from unreliable episodes using AUROC and best balanced accuracy. — RoboDrop §5.1, p. 6–7

Example

The 13 injected gripper_release episodes are the cleanest case of "ranked high but not at the top". Here is one, in the heuristic that finds it best (H8, post-release share minus the task median; artifacts/summary_examples2.txt):

libero_spatial/pick_up_the_black_bowl_between_the_plate_and_the_ramekin_and_place_it_on_the_plate/demo_13
  label:   gripper_release, corrupted_frames [[57, 77]], final release at step 77 (0.875 of the episode)
  H8 score 0.230, rank 16 of 882        (highest score = most suspicious)
libero_spatial/pick_up_the_black_bowl_next_to_the_ramekin_and_place_it_on_the_plate/demo_1
  label:   gripper_release, corrupted_frames [[97, 117]]
  H8 score 0.127, rank 29 of 882

Over all 13 injected episodes the ranks are 16, 17, 20, 23, 26, 27, 28, 29, 39, 48, 50, 78, 97. All 13 sit inside the top 100 of 882, and the highest-scoring injected one has 15 clean episodes above it (task-relative post-release shares of 0.24–0.57, measured by the analyzer). That is why the AUROC is 0.964 while recall@13, the share of injected episodes inside the top 13, is 0.000.

Divergences and limitations

  • n is small. 13 injected episodes per type: one episode moves recall by 7.7 points. No confidence intervals are given; n accompanies every number.
  • Directions were fixed before scoring, but the heuristics were designed by people who knew the corruptions. The analyzer's note: H1–H6 were designed the same way, so H7 and H8 are the same standard, not a stricter one. Treat the table as a floor for what is findable, not a ceiling.
  • Owner-approved limitations (ruling A). B: provenance stays visible and action noise is independent per step (found by the instruction cross-check and by smoothness). C: pauses and dropped frames repeat stored frames, so they are exact duplicates (found by H5). D: task-normalised length and release position find detour, idle_padding, time_shift and gripper_release at 0.85–0.96. Not re-opened.
  • Pooled recall is not comparable across types: the best single heuristic reaches 0.335 pooled recall@91 (H5) because it saturates two types outright.
  • Tie rule (tied scores ranked in random order, counted fractionally at the cut) is inferred; the PoC and RoboDrop state none. It matters for H5, H6 and idle steps, which produce large ties.
  • Real MLflow store. The score runs went to the real data/lineage/mlflow.db (for v3: 6 + 3 screen runs and 6 + 3 score runs over rounds 2 and 3, plus 6 + 6 earlier for the fenced v2, because AC5.6 requires the trace to reach the scored version). Its sha256 checkpoints are in the execution log.
